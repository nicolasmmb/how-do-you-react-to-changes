How do you react
to changes?

A story about leaving home, losing my bike, and learning to choose change.

Opening thought

The only constant in life is change.

attributed to Heraclitus

Heraclitus. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

BEAT: "Heraclitus said everything flows and nothing stays the same. Change is not an exception in life: it is the rule."
Part 1 · Talk about myself

From Fortaleza to Campos do Jordão

I was born in Fortaleza, Ceará, and lived there until I was 13 or 14. When my parents' jobs changed, my whole family moved to a small, cold mountain town in São Paulo — the highest city in Brazil.

Fortaleza, Ceará, my first home. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

Campos do Jordão, SP, where I grew up. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

BEAT: Ordinary world + the call: "I was born in Fortaleza and I used to live there until I was about thirteen. Then family moved to Campos do Jordão, a small cold mountain town. It was a huge contrast. That was my first big change, and I did not choose it."
Part 1 · My teenage years

Volleyball, my bike, and one accident

I used to play volleyball for my school team.

Riding my bike in the mountains.

At 21, a bike accident, two days before my birthday. The doctors told me I must stop riding.

BEAT: Inciting incident: "I used to play volleyball and ride my bike almost every day. That was my freedom. Then, at twenty-one, two days before my birthday, I had a bike accident and the doctors told me I must stop riding. Some changes arrive without permission. You only control how you react."

Part 2 · Define a successful change

Change is emotional before it is practical

01

Fear

The future is unknown, and we feel we are losing control.

02

Anxiety

We want quick results and certainty now.

03

Insecurity

We are afraid of failing in front of others.

04

What I feel: doubt

"Am I making the right decision?" even when I already know the answer.

"Most people feel fear, anxiety and insecurity when something new begins. In my case, I usually feel doubts: I ask myself if I am making the right decision, even when I already know the answer. I might feel nervous, but I can still move forward."
Part 2 · Emotions matter

Emotions are data

If you feel something bad about a change, it probably means it is not something you really want.

If you feel something good, it is the opposite.

So emotions should not be ignored, they must be understood. A change that goes against our emotions usually cannot last long.

The Kübler-Ross curve of emotions. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

"Emotions are real data. If you feel something bad about a change, it probably means it is not something you really want, and the opposite is also true. Emotions should not be ignored. They must be understood."
Part 2 · My definition

What is a successful change?

1

It was concluded

What was proposed actually happened.

2

I adapted well

The new situation became natural.

3

The metrics held

They showed that I did not regress.

4

It is marked done

I can close the chapter with confidence.

Also: change happens when it is imposed on you or when you want a new place. But if we never change, we never grow.

"A successful change is when what was proposed was concluded, when I adapted well, when the metrics held, and when I can mark it as done. Change happens when it is imposed, or when you want a new place. There is one real reason to change: not to stagnate."
Part 3 · A drastic change

My biggest change was professional

2018 – 2019

Apprentice at Minalba

Small routine, simple job, while I was studying IT at IFSP and already loved programming.

2021

Developer at PontoTel

Growing step by step, Junior II → Junior IV → mid-level. But comfort became the problem.

2025

Senior Developer at BRQ

I left a stable job for a bigger challenge. Focus: better opportunities and bigger companies.

Today · Hybrid

Senior Developer at B3

Work that can impact, and even cause the loss of, millions of reais or dollars.

I was feeling two things at once: apprehension and motivation. Insecurity always comes with something new, but I could not stay still.

BEAT: Conflict and turning point: "My most drastic change was professional. Apprentice at Minalba → developer at PontoTel → I felt I had reached a ceiling → in 2025 I left a stable job for BRQ → today I am at B3, where my work can impact millions. Apprehension and motivation at the same time, but I could not stay still."
Part 4 · Conclusion

So, how do I react to changes?

Positively, although with doubts and insecurity at the beginning.

I am adapting to B3, improving my English and building better habits. In the future I will pay off my house, start a postgraduate and reach a higher level of English. I might even look for an international opportunity, but I must prepare first.

B3, the Brazilian stock exchange. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

BEAT: Resolution + answer: "I am adapting to B3, improving my English, creating better habits. I will finish paying my house and start a postgraduate. I react to changes positively, with doubts at the beginning, because a change is only truly successful when the goal is concluded and the metrics show that I did not regress. I can choose change, and I will keep choosing it."
