How do you react
to changes?

A story about leaving home, losing my bike, and learning to choose change.

HOOK (open with the story, not the summary): "When I was thirteen, I was standing in the living room of the only house I knew, and my parents told me we were moving two thousand kilometers away. Opening thought

The only constant in life is change.

attributed to Heraclitus

Heraclitus. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

"Heraclitus said that everything flows and nothing stays the same. That's exactly the starting point of my lesson: change is not an exception in life: it is the rule."
Part 1 · Talk about myself

From Fortaleza to Campos do Jordão

I was born in Fortaleza, Ceará, and lived there until I was 13 or 14. When my parents' jobs changed, my whole family moved to a small, cold mountain town in São Paulo — the highest city in Brazil.

Fortaleza, Ceará, my first home. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

Campos do Jordão, SP, where I grew up. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

BEAT 1: Ordinary world + the call: "Before that day, my life was simple and predictable. I was born in Fortaleza, in the Northeast, and I used to live there until I was about thirteen. Then my parents' jobs changed everything: my family moved to Campos do Jordão, a small and cold mountain town. It was a huge contrast. That was my first big change, and honestly I did not choose it. If the Northeast taught me to be open, the mountains taught me to adapt."
Part 1 · My teenage years

Volleyball, my bike, and one accident

I used to play volleyball for my school team. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

Riding my bike in the mountains.

At 21, a bike accident, two days before my birthday. The doctors told me I must stop riding.

BEAT 2: Inciting incident (the moment I lost control): "As a teenager I used to play volleyball for my school team and I used to ride my bike almost every day. That was my freedom. Then, when I was twenty-one, two days before my birthday, I had a bike accident, and the doctors told me I must stop riding. That accident taught me the hard truth of this lesson: some changes arrive without permission. The only thing you control is how you react."

Part 2 · Define a successful change

Change is emotional before it is practical

01

Fear

The future is unknown, and we feel we are losing control.

02

Anxiety

We want quick results and we want certainty now.

03

Insecurity

We are afraid of failing in front of others.

04

What I feel: doubt

"Am I making the right decision?" even when I already know the answer.

"In my opinion, most people feel fear, anxiety and insecurity when something new begins. In my own case, I usually feel doubts: I ask myself if I am making the right decision, even when I already know the answer. I might feel nervous, but I can still move forward."
Part 2 · Emotions matter

Emotions are data

If you feel something bad about a change, it probably means it is not something you really want.

If you feel something good, it is the opposite.

So emotions should not be ignored, they must be understood. A change that goes against our emotions usually cannot last long.

The Kübler-Ross curve of emotions. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

"I believe emotions are real data about us. If you feel something bad about a change, that probably means the change is not something you really want to happen, and the opposite is also true. So emotions should not be ignored. They must be understood, because a change that goes against our emotions usually cannot last long."
Part 2 · My definition

What is a successful change?

1

It was concluded

What was proposed actually happened.

2

I adapted well

The new situation became natural.

3

The metrics held

They showed that I did not regress.

4

It is marked done

I can close the chapter with confidence.

Also: change only happens when it is imposed on you or when you want to reach a new place, because we always look for the easiest path. But if we never change, we never grow.

"For me, a successful change is when what was proposed was concluded, when I adapted well, when the metrics showed that I did not regress, and when I can finally mark the change as concluded. And I should be honest: I don't think change is always necessary. It happens when it is imposed on you, or when you really want to reach a new place. But there is one real reason to change: not to stagnate."
Part 3 · A drastic change

My biggest change was professional

2018 – 2019

Apprentice at Minalba

Small routine, simple job, while I was studying IT at IFSP and already loved programming.

2021

Developer at PontoTel

I was growing step by step, Junior II → Junior IV → mid-level. But comfort became the problem.

2025

Senior Developer at BRQ

I left a stable job for a bigger challenge. Focus: better opportunities and bigger companies.

Today · Hybrid

Senior Developer at B3

Work that can impact, and even cause the loss of, millions of reais or dollars.

During the transition I was feeling two things at once: apprehension and motivation. There is always insecurity when you start something new, but I could not stay still.

BEAT 3: Conflict and turning point (the story climax): "My most drastic change was professional. First I was an apprentice at Minalba. Then, in 2021, I moved into the tech market at PontoTel, growing step by step, but I felt I had reached a ceiling. I was comfortable, and comfort was exactly the problem. So, in 2025, I took the leap: I left a stable job and accepted a Senior position at BRQ, and today I am at B3, where my work can impact millions. Why? Because my focus was always better opportunities and bigger companies. During the transition I was feeling apprehension and motivation at the same time. There is always insecurity when you start something new, but I could not stay still."
Part 4 · Conclusion

So, how do I react to changes?

Positively, although with doubts and insecurity at the beginning.

I am adapting to B3, improving my English and building better habits. In the future I will pay off my house, start a postgraduate and reach a higher level of English. I might even look for an international opportunity, but I must prepare first.

B3, the Brazilian stock exchange. Source: Wikimedia Commons.

BEAT 4: Resolution + answer: "Am I currently changing anything? Yes: I am adapting to B3, I am improving my English, and I am creating better habits. In the next few years I will finish paying my house and start a postgraduate. Based on everything I have lived, I react to changes positively, although with doubts and insecurity at the beginning, because a change is only truly successful when the goal is concluded and the metrics show that I did not regress. Looking back at the boy who did not want to leave Fortaleza, I see that the change I feared the most gave me the most. I should not wait for change to be imposed on me. I can choose it, and I will keep choosing it, because not changing means not growing."
Closing

All changes, even the most longed for, have their melancholy; for what we leave behind us is a part of ourselves.

Anatole France
